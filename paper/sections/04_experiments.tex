\section{Experimental Setup}

\subsection{Datasets and Annotation Protocols}
To assess the generalizability and robustness of the proposed matching architecture without risk of insular evaluation, experiments are conducted across distinct benchmarks:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{VietResume-Bench (Open-Science Bilingual Benchmark)}: To ensure absolute reproducibility and eliminate reliance on proprietary company repositories, we construct and release \texttt{VietResume-Bench} from open Hugging Face corpora: $1,585$ authentic candidate CVs from \texttt{lhoestq/\allowbreak{}resumes-raw-pdf-for-ocr} ($574$ IT profiles alongside $1,011$ cross-domain distractor negatives) and $60$ detailed Vietnamese IT job descriptions from \texttt{tinixai/\allowbreak{}vietnamese-job-descriptions} spanning $10$ core technical domains ($6$ JDs per specialization: Java, Python/AI, React/Frontend, NodeJS/Backend, PHP/Web, Mobile, Data/BI, DevOps/Cloud, QA/Tester, BA).
    
    Relevance judgments ($\mathcal{Q}_{rels}$) span $60$ bidirectional queries ($60$ $\text{JD}\rightarrow\text{CV}$ and $60$ $\text{CV}\rightarrow\text{JD}$) assessed under a standardized $3$-tier graded rubric: Grade 0 (\textit{No Fit}), Grade 1 (\textit{Potential Fit}), and Grade 2 (\textit{Good Fit}), yielding $3,000$ validated candidate-job pairs.
    
    \textbf{Linguistic Stratification}: Candidate resumes are stratified into three distinct linguistic groups:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item $\text{VI}\leftrightarrow\text{VI}$: Monolingual Vietnamese CVs queried by Vietnamese JDs ($859$ candidates, $54.2\%$).
        \item $\text{VI}\leftrightarrow\text{EN}$: Cross-lingual pairing where Vietnamese JDs query English CVs ($346$ candidates, $21.8\%$).
        \item \textit{Code-Mixed}: Profiles characterized by Vietnamese prose interwoven with English technical entities ($380$ candidates, $24.0\%$).
    \end{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Public International Benchmark}: To provide an independent, unbiased external validation and eliminate concerns of circularity, we evaluate on the public benchmark by \cite{huggingface_resume_fit} (\texttt{resume-job-description-fit}) comprising $1,759$ test candidate-job pairs spanning $71$ unique job queries and $477$ candidate resumes. Relevance ground-truth is independently categorized into $3$ tiers: \textit{No Fit} (Grade 0), \textit{Potential Fit} (Grade 1), and \textit{Good Fit} (Grade 2).
    
    \item \textbf{Large-Scale Vietnamese Benchmark}: To assess vocabulary coverage, structured section availability, and scalability on macroscopic labor market data in emerging economies, we evaluate cross-market transfer using the Tinix Vietnam Job Description corpus~\cite{tinix2026vietnamese} ($606,878$ structured job postings across 2022--2026 covering $14$ recruitment facets).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Baseline Configurations and Fairness Controls}
We benchmark eight distinct algorithmic variants, with strict linguistic fairness controls:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Baseline 1 (BM25 / TF-IDF)}: Lexical retrieval using Okapi BM25. To guarantee a fair baseline for Vietnamese text rather than penalizing lexical retrieval through naive whitespace splitting, text is segmented into compound words via \texttt{pyvi} (\textit{e.g.}, ``lap\_trinh\_vien'', ``co\_so\_du\_lieu''). We also report comparative results with unsegmented whitespace tokenization to quantify the baseline penalty.
    \item \textbf{Baseline 2 (Single-Vector)}: Dense bi-encoder Cosine similarity over full document embedding $\mathbf{v}_{full}$.
    \item \textbf{Baseline 3 (Chunk Max-Sim)}: Documents segmented into overlapping 64-token chunks; score computed via maximum pairwise token similarity.
    \item \textbf{Baseline 4 (Cross-Encoder)}: Deep cross-attention scoring via \texttt{ms-marco-}\allowbreak\texttt{MiniLM-L-6-v2}.
    \item \textbf{Section-Aware (Fixed)}: Same-named section matching with mean aggregation.
    \item \textbf{Section-Aware (Cross)}: Cross-section maximal pairing with mean aggregation.
    \item \textbf{Section-Aware (Weighted)}: Cross-section pairing with non-uniform domain weights.
    \item \textbf{Hybrid \& Full Cascade}: Hybrid fusion ($\alpha \cdot S_{sem} + (1-\alpha) \cdot S_{rules}$) with dynamic weight renormalization and post-hoc local LLM reranking.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Disclosed Embedding Models}: We strictly distinguish model application by benchmark domain: multilingual models (\texttt{paraphrase-\allowbreak{}multilingual-MiniLM-L12-v2}, $384$-d, and \texttt{BAAI/\allowbreak{}bge-m3}, $1024$-d) are deployed for the bilingual \texttt{VietResume-Bench}, whereas \texttt{all-MiniLM-L6-v2} ($384$-d) is evaluated on the monolingual English public benchmark to prevent cross-dataset distortion.

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics and Significance Testing}
Following standard TREC/RecSys conventions, we evaluate ranking accuracy using nDCG@5, nDCG@10, Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR), Mean Average Precision (MAP), Recall@10, and Spearman rank correlation ($\rho$). To verify that performance gains are statistically meaningful and not artifacts of query sampling variance, pairwise differences are evaluated via two-tailed \textbf{paired $t$-tests} and \textbf{Wilcoxon signed-rank tests} at significance level $\alpha = 0.01$. We further report $95\%$ empirical confidence intervals derived from $1,000$ bootstrap resamples.

\subsection{Reproducibility and Execution Environment}
Experiments execute entirely on standard commodity hardware (Intel Core i7 CPU, 16\,GB RAM) without requiring GPU accelerators. Dense representations are produced via \texttt{BAAI/bge-m3} ($1024$-d) and \texttt{all-MiniLM-L6-v2} ($384$-d). Vector indexing is handled by ChromaDB (HNSW, space=cosine, $M=16$, $\textit{efConstruction}=100$). The local LLM reranker utilizes Qwen 4B / DeepSeek via Ollama with greedy decoding ($T=0$) enforcing strict JSON schemas.
