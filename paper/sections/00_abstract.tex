\begin{abstract}
Matching candidate resumes (Curriculum Vitae, CV) with Job Descriptions (JD) is a foundational component of digital talent acquisition and freelance marketplaces. Traditional automated screening systems rely predominantly on lexical keyword matching or flat single-vector dense representations. However, flat bi-encoders compress multi-page resumes into a single vector while enforcing strict context window limits ($256$--$512$ tokens), truncating late-document project experience and diluting critical technical competencies. Furthermore, practical hiring requires asymmetric prerequisite enforcement (mandatory vs.\ preferred skills) and auditable score explanations that black-box neural rankers fail to provide. In this paper, we propose a CPU-efficient, training-free framework for bidirectional CV--Job matching based on \textbf{Section-Aware Multi-Vector Representation} and a \textbf{Calibrated Hybrid Ranking Engine}. Our method segments documents into functional sections (skills, experience, education, summary) via a leak-free deterministic parser, encodes each section independently using frozen bi-encoders, and computes cross-section alignment with dynamic weight renormalization. These multi-vector semantic signals are fused with BM25 lexical matching and asymmetric requirement coverage over a 3-tier ontology of $615$ structured IT competencies. Extensive evaluation across two public benchmarks---\textsc{resume-job-description-fit} ($1,759$ test pairs, $71$ JD queries; plus $76$ clean held-out train queries) and the high-density \textsc{med2425} corpus ($8,575$ pairs across $41$ JDs and $277$ CVs)---yields three key findings: (1)~while Section-Aware Dense matches Single-Vector on pooled averages, length-stratified analysis reveals a monotonically expanding advantage on long resumes ($\Delta\text{nDCG@10}$ rising to $+0.0239$ on CVs $>600$ words, eliminating tail truncation on $>95\%$ of profiles) and statistically significant gains in reverse job recommendation ($\text{CV} \rightarrow \text{JD}$, $\bar{\Delta} = +0.0185$ nDCG@10, $p = 0.0022$); (2)~in high-density pools ($\approx 209$ CVs/JD), Calibrated Hybrid achieves significant improvements over Single-Vector in both candidate screening ($\text{JD} \rightarrow \text{CV}$: $\bar{\Delta} = +0.0849$ nDCG@10, $+15.7\%$, $p = 0.0211$) and job recommendation ($\text{CV} \rightarrow \text{JD}$: $\bar{\Delta} = +0.0855$ nDCG@10, $p < 0.0001$); and (3)~on commodity CPUs ($N=25$), Calibrated Hybrid executes candidate scoring in $T_{\text{rank}} = 2.69$\,ms ($T_{\text{online}} = 33.44$\,ms end-to-end including query encoding), significantly outperforming zero-shot Cross-Encoder reranking ($\bar{\Delta} = +0.0632$ nDCG@10, $p_{\text{RQ}} = 0.0082$) at $35\times$ lower latency ($1,178.05$\,ms).

\end{abstract}
