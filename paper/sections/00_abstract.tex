\begin{abstract}
Matching candidate resumes (Curriculum Vitae, CV) with Job Descriptions (JD) is a critical operational component of modern talent acquisition and freelance marketplaces. Traditional automated recruitment systems predominantly rely on either lexical keyword matching or flat single-vector dense representations. However, single-vector bi-encoders compress entire multi-page documents into an unweighted embedding, diluting fine-grained competencies across distinct sections such as technical skills, work experience, and educational background. Furthermore, practical recruitment demands structured domain rules (such as mandatory vs. preferred skill coverage and experience thresholds) that semantic similarity alone cannot satisfy. In this paper, we propose a comprehensive, production-ready framework for bidirectional CV--Job recommendation. Our method decomposes documents into functional sections, embeds each section independently into a multi-vector space via frozen bi-encoders, and computes section-level alignment using cross-section pairing with dynamic weight renormalization. To integrate domain expertise, a hybrid fusion engine combines multi-vector semantic representations, BM25 lexical relevance, and a 3-tier hierarchical skill taxonomy with asymmetric coverage. Extensive empirical evaluation on the international benchmark \textsc{resume-job-description-fit} ($1,759$ labeled candidate-job pairs across $71$ job queries) demonstrates that our Section-Aware Hybrid pipeline achieves a Mean Average Precision (MAP) of $0.6366$ on all queries ($0.7410$ on filtered positive queries, exceeding BM25 by $+7.3\%$ relative gain), an nDCG@10 of $0.6162$, and consistent rank correlation ($\rho_{\text{mac}} = 0.2954$, pooled $\rho_{\text{mic}} = 0.1944$). Crucially, candidate ranking latency requires $T_{\text{rank}} = 3.48$\,ms on commodity multi-core CPUs for candidate pool $N=25$ ($T_{\text{rank}} = 0.15$\,ms for Section-Aware Dense), with total end-to-end online query latency of $T_{\text{online}} \approx 23.7$\,ms including online query encoding ($T_{\text{query}} \approx 20.2$\,ms)---substantially faster than zero-shot Cross-Encoder forward passes ($1,174.67$\,ms). Under multiplicity-controlled testing, we find that while the hybrid pipeline achieves the highest MAP ($0.6366$), single-vector dense retrieval remains statistically competitive on top ranks, offering the most computationally economical deployment when ontology rules are optional.

\end{abstract}
